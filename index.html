Analytical Exposition Text

Give your opinion. Give your reasons.

What is it?

Analytical = uses reasons and facts

We explain WHY we think something.

Exposition = explaining an idea

We present our idea clearly to readers.

Social function (the purpose)

To persuade readers that a topic is important, by giving arguments.

Formula

Opinion + reasons + facts = persuasion

Think first, then click.

Text Structure: 3 Parts

1. Thesis

Say your opinion about the topic.

Example:
“Students should use Zoom for learning.”

2. Arguments

Give reasons and evidence.

Example:
“First, Zoom saves time. Second, it is easy to use.”

3. Reiteration

Say your opinion again, in other words.

Example:
“So, Zoom is a good tool for students.”

Analytical exposition = Thesis → Arguments → Reiteration

Example Text: “Students Should Study with Zoom”

I think students should study with Zoom.

THESIS: the writer’s opinion

First, Zoom saves time. Students do not need to travel to school.

ARGUMENT 1: reason + fact

Second, Zoom is easy to use. We can talk, see our friends, and share the screen.

ARGUMENT 2: another reason

In conclusion, Zoom is a good tool for learning. Therefore, students should use it.

REITERATION: the opinion again

Language Features

Simple present
facts and opinions

Zoom saves time. Students learn at home.

Linking verbs
be, feel, look, sound, seem

Zoom is easy. The class sounds fun.

Connectives & conjunctions
First, Second, Therefore, because, and, but

First, … Second, … Therefore, … Zoom is useful because it saves time.

Emotive words
show strong feeling

Zoom is amazing! It is very useful. The signal is terrible.

Emotive word: wonderful. Linking verb: is.
